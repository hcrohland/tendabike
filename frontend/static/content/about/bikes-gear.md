# Your gear

TendaBike is built around the things you ride. The biggest of them is your
bike: it's the top-level thing in the app, and everything you maintain —
wheels, tires, the chain, the brakes — hangs off it. The app calls this
collection **Your gear**, and it puts your bikes front and centre on the home
page, under the **Bikes** tab.

This page is about the bikes themselves: how they get here, what their card
tells you, how to change a bike's details, and what to do when you're finished
with one. The parts on a bike are covered in
[The components](/about/parts-assemblies).

## Your bikes are pulled from your account

You don't type your bikes in by hand. They come from the service where you log
your rides — Strava or intervals.icu — the same account you connected in
[Getting started](/about/onboarding).

When you first connect, the welcome dialog shows the bikes it found on your
account under **Your gear**. That's your first look at them. From then on, the
**Bikes** page keeps them up to date on its own: a bike you add on your account
shows up here, and you never have to import it by hand.

If the **Bikes** page is empty, TendaBike hasn't found a bike on your account
(yet). Make sure your gear is set up on the service side — in Strava, that your
bike is on your profile and your rides are assigned to it — and it'll appear.

## The Bikes page

The home page — the **Bikes** tab — is a grid of cards, one per bike, most
recently used first. Each card shows:

- **Name** — what you call the bike; click it to open the bike's page.
- **Model** — the brand and model, or **unknown model** if your account doesn't
  have one.
- A small provider icon that links back to your service (hover for **View on
  Strava** or **View on intervals.icu**) so you can jump to the bike where it's
  set up.
- **since** the date you got it.
- A row of small counters — rides, hours, kilometres, and climbing — for how
  much you've ridden on it.
- A badge that shows how close the bike's services are to being due: green,
  then amber, then red.

Clicking a card opens the bike's page, where you can see everything on it and
manage its services. It has four tabs:

- **Attached Parts** — the parts on the bike (see [The
  components](/about/parts-assemblies)).
- **Service Plans** and **Service Logs** — the services you schedule and the
  ones you've done (see [Planned maintenance](/about/service-plans)).
- **Notes** — free-form notes, with an optional attached file.

## Change a bike's details

Rename a bike, fix its brand and model, or correct the date you got it. Open
the bike, then in the **⋮** menu at the top right of its card, choose
**Change details**.

The dialog is titled **Change Bike details** and has three fields:

- **You call it** — the name.
- **and it is a** — the **Brand** and **Model**.
- **New Bike day was** — the date you got it.

Fill them in and click **Change**.

## When you're done with a bike

You don't delete a bike in TendaBike — you **Dispose** of it, the app's word
for "I'm no longer using this one." Open the bike, choose **Dispose** from the
**⋮** menu, set the date in the dialog, and click **Dispose**.

The bike moves out of the main grid into a **disposed** section at the bottom
of the **Bikes** page. It's not gone: open it and choose **Recover gear** to
bring it back, should you change your mind.

## Switching sport

TendaBike organises your gear by sport. The **Bikes** tab and the home page are
for bikes; the same app tracks skis, shoes, and other gear under their own
sport. To switch, open your user menu (top right) and choose **Switch Sport**.
The whole app follows — the tab, the home page, and the parts that hang off
each sport's gear.

## Example

You've ridden your gravel bike for a couple of years and it's showing its age.
You open the **Bikes** page, click *Gravel Bike*, and its card reads
**since 2024** with a red badge — a service is due. You open **Service Plans**,
log the service, and the badge clears.

Later you sell the bike. You open it, choose **Dispose**, and set the date; it
drops into the **disposed** section. A few months later you buy a replacement
and add it to your Strava profile. It shows up on the **Bikes** page on its
own, and you start tracking it like any other.
